Setting the Verrier telescope by hand

Field notes · Anaïs Berger · Observatory of Haute-Provence

The mount arrived in eleven crates with no manual. We had typed pages from 1962, hand-drawn plates, and two former students who remembered the drive. The first evening went to the counterweights, the second to the polar axis, about which the mount turns to follow the sky.1

Alignment is a loop, not a step. You point at a star near the meridian, you correct in azimuth; you point at a star near the horizon, you correct in altitude; then you begin again, because each correction spoils a little of the other. The typed pages call this chasing the residual, and give a tolerance of δ < 30″ before the drive can hold a plate for an hour. Our fourth night ended at 22″.

A telescope is not aimed. It is persuaded, slowly, into agreement with the Earth.

Hélène Verrier, Notes on Equatorial Mounts, 1958

The working procedure we settled on, after four nights, runs as follows:

  1. Level the pier and lock the clutches. The bubble reads to arcmin, which is enough at this stage.
  2. Set the latitude scale to 43°55′ and tighten in two passes:
    • a light pass by hand, felt rather than measured;
    • a second pass with the C-spanner, quarter turn on each bolt in a star pattern.
  3. Photograph a field near the celestial equator for 300 s and measure the trail with the eyepiece reticle.2

Two conventions from the old pages have survived unchanged. Temperatures go in the log as T = 4.5 °C, and every reading of the drive rate is recorded as a fraction of the sidereal rate, so r0 is the nominal rate and r1 the rate we set. The correction we apply on a cold night, 2.1 × 10−4, has been in the folder since November 1971. What we did change is the log itself: the old sheets said observer initials only, and we now write the full name and the seeing estimate.

On the control box, F1 releases the clutch, F2 engages the drive, and Shift doubles the slew rate. The handwritten label DO NOT SLEW WITH DOME CLOSED is still the instrument’s only warning.

1 The mount is a German equatorial, cast in Marseille.

2 The reticle divisions are 15″ at the prime focus. A trail shorter than one division was our working definition of success.
