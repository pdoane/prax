The last mill on the Ourthe still grinds by water

Two families, one wheel, and a licence that has to be renewed every seven years.
By Margit Salomon · Photographs by Tomas Rye · 14 minute read

The wheel turns eleven times a minute. Water follows a channel cut in 1740, drops onto the buckets and leaves through a quiet tailrace. The loudest sound is the gearing overhead.

Marthe Colline runs the mill four days a week and teaches the other three. She learned stone dressing from her father. Every six weeks the stones are dressed; between dressings the flour warms enough for local bakers to notice.

Nobody keeps a mill for the money. You keep it because the valley notices when it stops.

The seven-year licence covers the weir, fish pass and low-flow water allowance. Three offices review it. In 2018 they took fourteen months, leaving the mill idle for a summer.

Eleven bakeries buy the flour in twenty-five kilo sacks at prices close to the industrial mills. Marthe delivers on Tuesdays and telephones first when low water delays a run.

The oak beams will outlast the licence. The roof dates from 2009; the wheel has been replaced twice in three centuries, most recently in steel.

Reported from Comblain-au-Pont over four days in March.
