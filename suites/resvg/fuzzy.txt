# Fuzzy ranges that judge a case in place of the suite's limits, in the form of a fuzzy meta's content (web-platform-tests
# docs, "Fuzzy Matching"). An entry is a line of the case and its ranges for each case it covers, then the indented
# reason: why the engine's render differs from the reference by more than the limits allow, and why that is not an
# error. The references are resvg's renders, so a difference here is between two renderers that both conform. An entry
# whose content starts with shift=1 is measured with the drawing free to move by up to a pixel
# (countShiftedDifferences), which tolerates an edge that the renderers place a fraction of a pixel apart, and only that.

    An feOffset of 5 user units moves its input by 7.5 target pixels at this scale. The engine moves it by exactly that,
    so each tile's offset flood covers its first row and column by half and its corner by a quarter. The reference
    moves it by whole pixels and covers them fully. No specification says how an offset meets the pixel grid.

    filter-effects-1/#feGaussianBlurElement lets a large deviation be approximated by three box blurs, and both
    renderers do, with different boxes, so their falloffs differ wherever a blur fades. The difference is largest where

    alpha 136 a pixel inside it, where the Gaussian gives 136 and the reference 79.

    A pattern tile that starts between pixels is drawn snapped to whole pixels, and the two renderers snap it in
    opposite directions, so a one-pixel column or row at each tile's edge differs. Neither covers the edge pixel by the
    fraction that the tile does, and no specification says how a tile meets the pixel grid.

painting/shape-rendering/on-horizontal-line.svg shift=1;maxDifference=0-1;totalPixels=0-330
    The crisp lines are 1.5 target pixels thick and span rows 144.0 to 145.5, so the second row's center lies on the
    bottom edge. The engine's aliased fill takes a center on a top edge and leaves one on a bottom edge, as the usual
    fill convention does, and covers one row. The reference covers both. Crisp edges ask only for edges on whole pixels
    (SVG2/painting.html#ShapeRenderingProperty), and a tie may round either way.

    Each glyph on a curved path is placed and turned at its own point of the path, which the renderers find within a
    fraction of a pixel of each other, and a turned glyph's edges cover their pixels by different fractions as it moves.
    No glyph differs by more than that. On m-A-path, the engine's render differs from Chrome's in 1181 pixels and from
    Firefox's in 971, and the reference differs from them in 1416 and 1216: the browsers are closer to the engine than
    to the reference. The underline along the path lies 0.6 pixels lower than the reference's, and the doubled-back
    stroke of the M L Z path meets itself in partly covered pixels, which the engine's rasterizer covers by its area
    once. A stretched glyph is bent onto the path by a warp that each renderer approximates in its own way, so a few
    pixels of its outline differ by more than a shift.

text/letter-spacing/mixed-scripts.svg maxDifference=0-255;totalPixels=0-2600
    The middle-anchored line is drawn 2 pixels left of the reference. The browsers spread over 3 pixels about the
    engine: Chrome sets the line 1 pixel right of it and Firefox 2 pixels left. Each glyph, and each gap between them,
    matches the reference to the pixel, so only where the line's middle falls differs.
